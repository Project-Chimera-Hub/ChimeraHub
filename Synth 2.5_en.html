📖 Grapheme–colour synesthesia trainer

Every letter and digit gets a colour (its identity) and a shape (its type). The goal is to move from conscious decoding — "square, red, that must be A" — to automatic recognition, where the colour arrives before the reasoning does.

🧠 What the research actually says
Bor, Rothen, Schwartzman, Clayton & Seth (2014), Scientific Reports, trained 14 adults on letter–colour associations for 9 weeks. The trained group showed synesthesia-like Stroop interference, and a mean IQ gain of about 12 points versus controls.

Read it with care: n = 14 trained participants, and the authors note the IQ result was not their primary outcome and needs replication. The synesthetic effects faded within three months of stopping. The reliable, repeatedly-measured finding is the one this app can actually check for you — the associations become automatic, which is what the Automaticity check measures.

🎮 Modes

    🎯 How the scheduler picks symbols

    Symbols are not drawn uniformly. Each one carries a priority built from four things:

    • Accuracy — the more often you miss it, the more often it returns.
    • Time since you last saw it — untouched symbols resurface (spacing effect).
    • Response speed — a symbol you get right but slowly is not yet automatic, so it keeps its priority.
    • Novelty — anything never seen goes to the front of the queue.

    This priority is saved between sessions, so closing the tab no longer resets what the app knows about you.

    ⏱️ Adaptive timer

    The time window is a weighted staircase that hunts for the setting where you score a target accuracy — 80% by default. A correct answer shrinks it by about 2.4%; a mistake grows it by 10%. Those two numbers are not arbitrary: for a staircase to settle at accuracy P, the step sizes must satisfy down / up = (1 − P) / P.

    What changed from 2.4.2: the old rule shrank the window 10% on a correct answer and grew it 10% on a miss. Equal steps settle wherever wins and losses balance — which for that pair is 47.5% accuracy. That is why the clock always ran away to something unplayable. It also reset to the full starting time every session, throwing away the calibration each time.
    • It is remembered. Your calibrated window is saved per mode and reloaded next time, with a 15% warm-up allowance on the first question of a session.
    • It is per-item. A symbol you have barely met gets 35% more room; one you have mastered (≥90% over 8+ sightings) gets 15% less, so mastered material keeps being pushed for speed.
    • The window has a floor and a ceiling — it can stretch to 2.5× the mode's base time if you need it, rather than being capped at the starting value.
    • The live Window readout in the drill header shows the current calibration.
    • Change the target in Tools → Preferences → Difficulty target, or switch the clock off entirely with Timed drills. Speed is still recorded either way.

    🌡️ The automaticity check (Stroop)

    A letter is printed in a colour that either matches its trained colour or clashes with it. You name the ink colour and ignore the letter. If the association has become automatic you cannot ignore it — clashing trials get slower. That gap, in milliseconds, is your interference score, and watching it grow is the most honest evidence that the training is doing something.

    Give it at least ~30 trials before reading anything into the number. Below that it is mostly noise.

    💡 Practical advice

    • Short and daily beats long and occasional. Ten minutes a day is the whole method.
    • Start in Learn, then Decode, and only add Word/Phrase once single symbols feel effortless.
    • When you keep missing a pair, go to Confusions — mixed-up pairs need contrast, not more volume.
    • Export your progress occasionally. Local storage is not a backup.
