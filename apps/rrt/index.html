Running Order
Relational Reasoning Training

Learn a board of symbols, one per slot. Each card puts a new symbol a few steps from one you hold; it takes that slot and the symbol there leaves. Nothing else moves. Answer where the new one stands — before the next card arrives. Speed and load adapt to you.

First time here? The rules are short but they do not read well on paper. The walkthrough shows the order changing, one card at a time, with nothing timed.

Past one dimension every card places the symbol on every axis at once, and the card says which rank to report: ↕ height, ↔ longitude, ╱ latitude or ● size. Keep all of them — the next card may ask any. The first three are one drawn box, height up, longitude across and latitude into it; size is the fourth and sits outside the box, which is why depth is never shown by drawing a symbol smaller.

Pace
Session mode

15 minutes. Cards start 3 s apart and are visible for 0.4 s, each wiped by a masking screen the moment it goes; an episode opens with its first symbol alone for 1.5 s. The pace moves on every answer toward 80% above chance, between 0.8 s and 6 s. When the pace is at 0.8 s and you are still above target, the level rises and the pace relaxes; when it is at 6 s and you are still below, the level falls.

Accuracy above chance: 0% is guessing, 100% is perfect — so the target means the same at every size. The card is never shown for more than half the interval. The floor is where speed stops and the level takes over.

The encoding period is how long the board is shown at the start of an episode, per symbol on it — a board of five gets five times this. It is never shorter than one interval, and the board goes at 85% of it, so there is a real gap before the first card.

Adaptive level — raise or lower the level when the pace reaches its floor or ceiling

Off: the level stays where it starts and only the pace adapts.

Masking screen — a scatter of symbols over the card the moment it goes

On, the card is wiped by a scatter of the same kind of symbols, so the image does not linger on the retina and the order has to be in your head rather than in your eyes. Off, the card simply goes and a short afterimage is yours to use — easier, and a session run that way is not comparable with one that was masked.

Level
Symbols and dimensions

Generated marks never repeat, so nothing about a symbol can be learned in place of the order. That is the default and the honest measurement. Animals are a fixed pool of 34 drawings with names, so one lands in your head in a word and almost the whole beat goes on the order instead of on taking the symbol in — easier, and the way in if the marks are the part that defeats you. It also means the stimuli recur, and a fixed set is what automatises, so bits per second is not comparable between the two: sessions are tagged with the set they used and the best-ever figure is kept per set. Pictures go further: 56 colour photographs of everyday things — fruit, animals, vehicles, objects — each with a name you already know.

Speak the names — say each symbol's name aloud as it appears: the whole board at the start, then every new one. Only animals and pictures have names; the generated marks stay silent. Hearing the word as well as seeing the picture is a second way in, so a spoken session keeps its own best score.

The ladder: 1D with 3–7 symbols, then 2D with 3–5, then 3D with 3–5 — ordered by how much you carry, so every step up is more. Adding a dimension drops the symbol count back to 3.

Continue from last level — start where the previous session ended

Used when "continue" is off, or when there is no previous session.

Controls

Scales the rank keys. On a phone held upright the default keys are smaller than the fingertip aiming at them, and the neighbour of the key you meant is the rank next to the one you meant — the one mistake the task cannot tell from a real error.

A size that does not fit is given as much as does fit: the pad is the key times the number of keys, so a level with more symbols gets smaller keys than one with fewer, and on the height axis — where the keys stand in a column beside the card — it is the strip of width the card leaves over that decides. Keys are never made smaller than they were.
